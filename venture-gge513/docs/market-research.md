## Problem

People who need a specific capability cannot find the person *just ahead of them* —
someone who has already done the adjacent work, in public, and can both do the job
and pull them up. Directories, feeds, and talent marketplaces show claims (titles,
bios, "open to work"). They do not show what someone has actually shipped, and they
do not hand the asker a moment of judgment.

The sharing economy made idle *physical* capacity reachable (cars, rooms, machines).
The same move has not been made for human capability people want to share or donate.
The skill is latent. The person is not idle. Treating people as available inventory
is the wrong problem statement, and it is the one the category keeps writing.

**Who hurts:** an operator of a bounded group (an accelerator, a fellowship, a
residency, a professional association) who cannot point a visitor at the two or three
people whose work actually sits closest to the ask. A visitor with a real sentence
("I need a patient community app") who is currently told to browse a directory.

**How much:** the cost is missed connection, not a line item. The cohort instance
already measured one form of it: **1 of 31 builders claimed** their card, so
reachability — the thing a visitor actually needs — is almost entirely missing on
the supply side. That is an adverse datum, not a marketing problem to be spun.

**Evidence, not interviews:**

- George's own frame, held for years before the site existed (`THESIS.md` claim 1;
  Evernote "HemaCare Lessons", "Constant Learning", "Customers").
- Two independent peer reviews of the Week 3 build (@joes9987, @mitchelldante99-create)
  both said the match experience is the product and the hiring-directory brief is a
  different product. That is observer evidence that the demand-side job is real and
  that the assigned brief was the wrong customer.
- `/proof` as of 2026-08-14: **1 of 31 developed · 14 sent.** Fourteen visitors took
  a composed message away. One builder is reachable. The demand-side gesture exists;
  the supply-side consent does not yet match it.

## ICP

**Today (the live instance):** a visitor with one sentence about work they need built,
looking at one summer cohort of builders. Not a hiring partner evaluating a roster.
The Week 3 brief named that partner; the product does not serve that journey, and
two reviewers said so.

**If shape 1 is ruled:** the *buyer* is the operator of a bounded group that already
publishes work. The *user* is still the visitor with a sentence. Matching stays free.
The operator pays for the instance.

**Not the ICP:** patients and families. That is Support (`SUPPORT.md`). Support
inherits only what has survived contact in Universal. Selling it this week would
violate the ordering lock.

## Wedge

1. Match on public work, not on bios.
2. Explain the match, or it is a horoscope.
3. Hand the visitor the judgment moment; do not claim taste.
4. Reachability is opted in. Unclaimed is latent, and latent cannot be mailed.
5. Count actions, never people. The events table has no name column, so a
   leaderboard cannot exist even by accident.

The wedge dies if the substrate becomes self-authored claims (shape 2). That is
why the sitting is load-bearing and why this packet does not pretend it has happened.
