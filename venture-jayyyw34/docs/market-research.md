# Market research: LocalPlate

## Problem

Home cooks and bakers already move extra food through private social channels. Neighbors who want that food cannot see a live, local inventory. National delivery marketplaces are built for restaurants that can pay courier economics. The gap is a same-day neighborhood board with remaining servings and pickup windows — not another 30-minute courier promise.

Evidence of the informal channel is public and repeated: cottage-food Facebook groups, Instagram “preorder by Thursday” stories, Nextdoor “who wants empanadas” posts. Those are not LocalPlate interviews; they are the desk-research starting point.

## Market size

Figures are public-order-of-magnitude, not a commissioned TAM study.

- **TAM (desk):** USDA Economic Research Service publishes U.S. food-expenditure series in which food-away-from-home is a trillion-dollar-scale category. LocalPlate is not chasing all of that. TAM here means “Americans paying someone else to cook” — useful only as a ceiling. Source: [USDA ERS Food Expenditure Series](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-expenditure-series).
- **SAM (desk):** Informal homemade / cottage / shared-kitchen supply plus the buyers already hunting it on Instagram and Nextdoor, in U.S. metros with cottage-food statutes or accessible commercial kitchens. Cottage-food laws exist in nearly every state, with **large variation** in what may be sold (often baked goods; often not meals). Source: state cottage-food statutes; overviews such as Harvard Food Law / Forrager-style trackers (verify per state before onboarding a cook).
- **SOM (pilot assumption):** Greater Boston, walkable neighborhoods listed in the product (Cambridge, Somerville, Allston, Brookline, Jamaica Plain, Fenway, South End, Medford). Year-1 model in the business plan (~40 cooks) is an **assumption**, not a forecast from signed cooks.

Delivery-app take rates of roughly 15–30% are widely reported in restaurant-operator coverage of DoorDash and Uber Eats. That is the fee pressure LocalPlate is not trying to copy for six-plate menus.

## Competition

| Competitor | Strength | Weakness |
|------------|----------|----------|
| DoorDash | Liquidity, payments, brand | Courier cost; restaurant-shaped supply |
| Uber Eats | Same | Same |
| Shef | Homemade positioning | National marketplace gravity; slower “tonight” loop |
| Instagram / WhatsApp | Native cook behavior | No inventory or browse |
| Nextdoor | Neighborhood graph | Classifieds, not a menu |
| Facebook groups | Cottage-food communities | Spam, weak fulfillment |

## ICP

- **Cook:** Home baker or cook who already sells or gifts food socially; can operate under cottage-food rules **or** a licensed shared kitchen; wants 4–12 plates to move the same day; Greater Boston first.
- **Buyer:** Renter or student in a dense neighborhood who will walk 10–15 minutes for dinner; tired of delivery fees; willing to pre-reserve.
- **Not ICP (yet):** Full-service restaurants, nationwide shipping bakers, people who need ADA delivery, unpermitted hot-meal vendors we would be inducing to break local law.

## Wedge

Win **same-day, walkable inventory**. Incumbents win logistics and national SEO. LocalPlate wins the three-block radius where a cook can sell six plates without a car and a neighbor can eat food that still tastes like a house kitchen.
