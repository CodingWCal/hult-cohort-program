# Market research: Setu (working name)

## Problem

Rural and informal-economy households in India routinely earn near or below $2–3/day and have little buffer against small shocks — a sick cow, a slow work week, a medical bill. Without cheap, fast, small-scale credit or insurance, a shock that costs a few dollars can force a high-interest informal loan that starts a debt spiral (the "scarcity trap" described in behavioral-economics research on poverty). Existing formal credit is not sized for this: most digital lenders in India price and size loans for salaried urban borrowers, not sub-$10 emergency needs.

## Market size

- **TAM (rural credit + agri-fintech, India):** India's agri-fintech and rural credit market is valued at roughly ₹1,200 billion (~$14.4B) (Ken Research). Globally, the agri-fintech market was $7.29B in 2024, projected to reach $64.69B by 2035 at a 21.27% CAGR (industry market report, global figure — cited for growth-rate context, not as an India-specific number).
- **SAM (households reachable via existing rural finance rails):** NABARD's SHG-Bank Linkage Programme — the world's largest microfinance program by client base — had ~10 crore (100 million) women organized into ~91 lakh (9.1 million) self-help groups as of June 2025, with ~US$25B in annual loan disbursement (FY2024) and $7.8B in aggregate SHG savings. This is the realistic distribution channel (SHGs + MFI field agents), not a cold-start consumer app.
- **SOM (pilot-realistic, this cohort window):** A handful of villages/SHGs reachable through one MFI, NGO, or SHG federation partner — realistically dozens to low hundreds of households for a first pilot, not a market-wide launch.
- **Insurance angle:** 104.52 million of India's 195.6 million rural households own cattle (Swiss Re / PIB), but livestock-insurance penetration is only ~0.98% — a large, cited coverage gap for the shock-insurance product line.

## Competition

| Competitor | Focus | Strength | Weakness (relative to this idea) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jai Kisan** (Bharat Khata) | Rural/agri lending, now holds an NBFC (Kushal Finnovation) | 800K+ farmers reached, own NBFC license solves the regulatory problem | Loan-only; no bundled shock insurance or forced-savings product |
| **Samunnati** | Agri value-chain financing (inputs, post-harvest) | Deep agri-supply-chain integration | B2B/value-chain focused, not a direct consumer income-smoothing product |
| **Farmeasy Technologies** (Farm Infinity) | Loans + insurance for smallholder farmers | Already bundles credit and insurance | Farmer-only; doesn't cover non-farm daily-wage informal workers |
| **Tala** | Smartphone micro-loans, $10–500, alt-data credit scoring | Proven small-ticket model, entered India in 2025, 22K+ daily signups globally | General-purpose consumer credit; no insurance or savings-conversion bundle; loan floor ($10) is at the *top* of what this idea needs to go below |
| **Branch International** | Smartphone micro-loans, active in India | 4M+ users, profitable, established in Indian market | Same gap as Tala — credit-only, not a full scarcity-trap bundle |
| **Traditional MFIs** (CreditAccess Grameen, Bandhan, Spandana Sphoorty, Fusion) | Group-lending (JLG/SHG) microfinance | Massive existing rural distribution and trust | In-person/field-agent heavy, not app-first; loan sizes still typically larger than a $10 emergency need |
| **UIIC / PMFBY and other formal insurers** | Crop and livestock micro-insurance | Government-backed, subsidized premiums | Extremely low uptake (~1% livestock penetration) — distribution and claims friction, not product design, is the gap |

## ICP

Rural or peri-urban Indian household with informal/daily-wage or small-farm income (~$1–3/day equivalent), already reachable through an existing SHG, MFI, or NGO field relationship (not a cold digital-only acquisition target), who has faced at least one recent income shock (illness, livestock loss, lost work days) they covered with informal high-interest borrowing.

## Wedge

No single competitor combines all four pieces this idea proposes (sub-$10 loans, cheap shock micro-insurance, fee-into-savings loans, and lump-sum-to-monthly conversion) into one relationship with the same household. Credit players (Jai Kisan, Tala, Branch, MFIs) don't sell insurance or forced savings; insurers (UIIC, PMFBY) don't lend or smooth income; nobody offers the harvest-to-monthly conversion product at all. The wedge is bundling — using the existing SHG/MFI distribution trust layer (NABARD's ~100M-woman network) as the acquisition channel rather than competing on cold-start credit-scoring like Tala/Branch, and partnering with a licensed NBFC rather than seeking a license directly.
