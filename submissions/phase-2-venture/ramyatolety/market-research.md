# Market research: Setu (working name)

## Problem

Rural and informal-economy households in India routinely earn near or below $2 to $3 a day and have little buffer against small shocks: a sick cow, a slow work week, a medical bill. Without cheap, fast, small-scale credit or insurance, a shock that costs a few dollars can force a high-interest informal loan that starts a debt spiral (the "scarcity trap" described in behavioral-economics research on poverty). Existing formal credit is not sized for this. Most digital lenders in India price and size loans for salaried urban borrowers, not sub-$10 emergency needs.

## Market size

- **TAM (rural credit and agri-fintech, India):** India's agri-fintech and rural credit market is valued at roughly ₹1,200 billion (about $14.4B) (Ken Research). Globally, the agri-fintech market was $7.29B in 2024, projected to reach $64.69B by 2035 at a 21.27% CAGR (industry market report, a global figure, cited here for growth-rate context, not as an India-specific number).
- **SAM (households reachable via existing rural finance rails):** NABARD's SHG-Bank Linkage Programme, the world's largest microfinance program by client base, had about 10 crore (100 million) women organized into about 91 lakh (9.1 million) self-help groups as of June 2025, with about US$25B in annual loan disbursement (FY2024) and $7.8B in aggregate SHG savings. This is the realistic distribution channel, meaning SHGs and MFI field agents, not a cold-start consumer app.
- **SOM (pilot-realistic, this cohort window):** A handful of villages or SHGs reachable through one MFI, NGO, or SHG federation partner, realistically dozens to low hundreds of households for a first pilot, not a market-wide launch.
- **Insurance angle:** 104.52 million of India's 195.6 million rural households own cattle (Swiss Re, PIB), but livestock-insurance penetration is only about 0.98%, a large, cited coverage gap for the shock-insurance product line.

## Competition

| Competitor | Focus | Strength | Weakness (relative to this idea) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jai Kisan** (Bharat Khata) | Rural and agri lending, now holds an NBFC (Kushal Finnovation) | 800K+ farmers reached, own NBFC license solves the regulatory problem | Loan-only. No bundled shock insurance or forced-savings product |
| **Samunnati** | Agri value-chain financing (inputs, post-harvest) | Deep agri-supply-chain integration | B2B and value-chain focused, not a direct consumer income-smoothing product |
| **Farmeasy Technologies** (Farm Infinity) | Loans and insurance for smallholder farmers | Already bundles credit and insurance | Farmer-only. Doesn't cover non-farm daily-wage informal workers |
| **Tala** | Smartphone micro-loans, $10 to $500, alt-data credit scoring | Proven small-ticket model, entered India in 2025, 22K+ daily signups globally | General-purpose consumer credit. No insurance or savings-conversion bundle. The $10 loan floor sits at the top of what this idea needs to go below |
| **Branch International** | Smartphone micro-loans, active in India | 4M+ users, profitable, established in Indian market | Same gap as Tala: credit-only, not a full scarcity-trap bundle |
| **Traditional MFIs** (CreditAccess Grameen, Bandhan, Spandana Sphoorty, Fusion) | Group-lending (JLG and SHG) microfinance | Massive existing rural distribution and trust | In-person and field-agent heavy, not app-first. Loan sizes still typically larger than a $10 emergency need |
| **UIIC, PMFBY, and other formal insurers** | Crop and livestock micro-insurance | Government-backed, subsidized premiums | Extremely low uptake (about 1% livestock penetration). Distribution and claims friction, not product design, is the gap |

## ICP

A rural or peri-urban Indian household with informal daily-wage or small-farm income (roughly $1 to $3 a day equivalent), already reachable through an existing SHG, MFI, or NGO field relationship (not a cold digital-only acquisition target), that has faced at least one recent income shock (illness, livestock loss, lost work days) it covered with informal high-interest borrowing.

## Wedge

No single competitor combines all four pieces this idea proposes: sub-$10 loans, cheap shock micro-insurance, fee-into-savings loans, and lump-sum-to-monthly conversion, into one relationship with the same household. Credit players (Jai Kisan, Tala, Branch, MFIs) don't sell insurance or forced savings. Insurers (UIIC, PMFBY) don't lend or smooth income. Nobody offers the harvest-to-monthly conversion product at all. The wedge is bundling: using the existing SHG and MFI distribution trust layer (NABARD's roughly 100 million woman network) as the acquisition channel rather than competing on cold-start credit scoring the way Tala and Branch do, and partnering with a licensed NBFC rather than seeking a license directly.
