# Setu (working name), One-Pager

Exploratory draft, week one of a cohort pilot (Hult Cohort Program, Phase 2 "Venture"). "Setu" (Hindi for "bridge") is a placeholder name, not finalized.

## Problem
Rural and informal-economy households in India earn near or below $2 to $3 a day and have almost no buffer against small shocks: a sick cow, a slow work week, a medical bill. Without cheap, fast, small-scale credit or insurance, a shock costing a few dollars forces high-interest informal borrowing that spirals into debt (the "scarcity trap"). Formal digital credit in India is priced for salaried urban borrowers, not sub-$10 emergency needs. Livestock-insurance penetration is only about 0.98%, despite about 104.5 million rural households owning cattle (Swiss Re, PIB).

## Solution
A technology platform, not a direct lender, bundling four income-smoothing products around one household relationship, distributed through the SHG and MFI networks rural India already trusts:

1. **Emergency loans** sized for people earning about $1 a day, sub-$10, reasonable interest
2. **Shock micro-insurance** for sick livestock and fluctuating work hours
3. **Fee-into-savings loans**, where the fee funds a savings account in the borrower's name
4. **Harvest-to-stipend converter**, turning lump-sum harvest income into a predictable monthly stipend

No existing competitor bundles all four. The wedge is bundling plus trusted distribution, not cold-start credit scoring, the approach Tala and Branch use.

## Regulatory model
Real consumer lending in India requires RBI or NBFC registration, or operation as a **Lending Service Provider (LSP)** for a licensed NBFC under RBI's September 2022 digital lending guidelines. Setu is structured as the tech, origination, and servicing layer, partnering with a licensed NBFC (and a licensed insurer for the insurance line). It does not hold a lending license itself and does not move real money in this pilot.

## Market
- **TAM:** India's agri-fintech and rural credit market is about ₹1,200B (about $14.4B) (Ken Research)
- **SAM:** NABARD's SHG-Bank Linkage Programme, about 91 lakh SHGs, about 10 crore (100M) women, about $25B FY2024 disbursement, the realistic distribution rail
- **SOM:** one pilot MFI, NGO, or SHG-federation partnership, dozens to low hundreds of households
- The livestock-insurance coverage gap (about 0.98% penetration of about 104.5M cattle-owning households) sizes the insurance line specifically

## Business model
Fee and commission based, not balance-sheet lending: origination and servicing fees from the NBFC partner, insurance distribution commission, a platform fee on the savings-linked loan, and a conversion fee on the stipend product. Low-cost acquisition via existing SHG and MFI field relationships rather than paid digital acquisition.

## Traction (honest, week one)
No real users yet. What's real: the product concept and four-line bundle are defined, the regulatory (NBFC and LSP) framing is worked out, the distribution channel is identified and sized (NABARD SHG-BLP), and the competitive landscape is mapped against 7 players. Customer discovery (5 target interviews) has not yet been conducted, flagged as the immediate next step, not retrofitted.

## Team
Solo founder and builder currently, building this as a Hult Cohort Program Phase 2 pilot. Near-term gaps: an India-based compliance and legal lead for the NBFC partnership, and a field-ops lead with SHG and MFI relationships.

## Financials
Illustrative only (full model and assumptions in `business-plan.md`): about 150 households and about ₹30,000 (about $360) revenue in year 1, scaling to about 12,000 households and about ₹6,000,000 (about $72,000) by year 3, likely at or near breakeven at best. All are modeled planning assumptions, not results. Revenue is fee and commission based; Setu does not carry credit risk or hold deposits itself.

## The ask
**Illustrative: $150,000 to $250,000 pre-seed**, for NBFC and insurer partnership setup, MVP build, a first SHG-federation pilot, and customer discovery. Directional, not a firm modeled ask.
